ETHICS & OVERSIGHT

Responsible AI

EvalAssist is a tool to assist teachers, not to replace them. This page explains our commitments on oversight, fairness, privacy, known limitations, and when you should not use it.

Principles

Our core commitments

Human oversight

  • Every score is labelled provisional throughout the interface
  • A teacher must approve marks before they are used for any purpose
  • The teacher can edit any mark freely — EvalAssist never overrides a human decision
  • The approve/return workflow is built into the product, not optional
  • No mark is ever stored, shared, or surfaced to a student without explicit teacher action

Bias & accessibility

  • TF-IDF keyword matching can disadvantage students who paraphrase correctly but differ in vocabulary
  • Non-native English writers may be penalised for unconventional phrasing, even when accurate
  • Teachers should scrutinise scores for students from diverse linguistic backgrounds
  • The tool does not evaluate style, argument structure, or creative expression
  • Colour contrast ≥ 4.5:1; keyboard-navigable; respects prefers-reduced-motion

Privacy & data handling

  • EvalAssist runs entirely on your local machine — no data is sent to external servers
  • Nothing is logged, stored, or persisted between sessions
  • Session history exists only in RAM and is cleared when the server restarts
  • Always use anonymised data — replace names and IDs before uploading
  • Files are processed locally; they are never written to disk by the app

Transparency

  • The scoring formula (R = 0.65·S + 0.35·K) is shown in full for every criterion
  • Matched and missing keywords are listed as visible chips
  • The best-matching sentence is shown so teachers can verify the evidence
  • Weights (0.65, 0.35) are a starting heuristic — they are disclosed and changeable in source code
  • No accuracy percentages, benchmark comparisons, or performance claims are made
Limitations

Known limitations

Technical limitations

  • → TF-IDF doesn't understand meaning. A correct paraphrase without the exact keyword scores lower than expected.
  • → Keyword matching is brittle. Spelling mistakes, abbreviations, and synonyms are not handled.
  • → No OCR support. Scanned PDFs are detected and flagged, but cannot be marked.
  • → Feedback is template-based, not natural-language generated. It names missing words, not conceptual gaps.

Assessment limitations

  • → A high keyword-coverage score does not guarantee the answer is correct — keywords can appear in an incorrect context.
  • → The heuristic weights (0.65·S + 0.35·K) have not been validated against a dataset of human-marked answers.
  • → Scoring across multiple criteria is independent — it does not detect contradictions or logical inconsistencies.
  • → The tool does not evaluate academic integrity or detect plagiarism.

When NOT to use EvalAssist

  • High-stakes exams where marks determine progression, awards, or qualifications without mandatory human re-check
  • Handwritten answers (upload text only; handwriting recognition is not supported)
  • Highly subjective or open-ended essays without a well-defined rubric
  • Assessment of creative or argumentative writing where style and structure matter
  • Situations where the teacher cannot or does not review each provisional score

TRANSPARENCY

How EvalAssist works

A plain-language walkthrough of the pipeline — from raw text to a provisional mark that a teacher can approve.

Pipeline

The evaluation pipeline

Six stages, fully traceable. Every number in the result comes from one of these stages.

1 · Input

Answer text + rubric criteria + maximum marks

2 · Text extraction

TXT, PDF, or DOCX → plain UTF-8 text

3 · Features (TF-IDF)

Criterion and answer vectorised; cosine similarity computed per sentence

4 · Signals (S, K)

S = cosine similarity; K = keyword coverage share

5 · Score

R = min(1, 0.65·S + 0.35·K); marks = (M/n)·R

6 · Teacher review

Edit final marks; add comment; approve or return

Signals explained

S – Cosine Similarity

The answer and each criterion are converted into TF-IDF vectors. In Best-matching sentence mode, EvalAssist compares the criterion against every sentence in the answer and reports the highest similarity score and the sentence it came from. In Whole answer mode, the entire answer is compared at once.

Cosine similarity measures the angle between the vectors, not their length. Two texts that share the same important words will have a score closer to 1.0. Scores near 0 mean the texts are topically unrelated.

K – Keyword Coverage

EvalAssist extracts content words from each criterion (words longer than two characters, excluding common stop words, with light suffix stripping). It then checks which of those words appear somewhere in the student's answer.

K = matched / (matched + missing). The result panel shows each matched word as a green chip and each missing word as a red struck-through chip, so the evidence is always visible.

Formula

Scoring formula

R = min(1, 0.65 × S + 0.35 × K)
marks = (M / n) × R
M = maximum marks for the question
n = number of criteria
S = TF-IDF cosine similarity (0–1)
K = keyword coverage ratio (0–1)
R = combined ratio, capped at 1
Bands: R ≥ 0.75 → Strong coverage · 0.45–0.74 → Partial coverage · < 0.45 → Limited evidence. Feedback names up to four missing keywords.
⚠ The weights (0.65, 0.35) are a starting heuristic chosen for sensibility, not a statistically validated grading model. They should be reviewed against your own marking data before use in high-stakes assessment.

Teacher review

Every suggested mark is labelled provisional. The teacher can:

  • → Edit any criterion's final mark directly in the table
  • → Add a personalised written comment
  • → Approve the marks (copying feedback to clipboard)
  • → Return the submission for re-marking

Important limitations

  • TF-IDF does not understand meaning — a semantically correct paraphrase without the keyword will score lower than expected.
  • Keyword matching is brittle with spelling mistakes, abbreviations, or domain jargon.
  • Scanned PDFs produce almost no extractable text; OCR is not supported.
  • Feedback is template-based, not natural-language generated.
  • The tool is not designed for highly subjective essays or open-ended creative work.
